The full-load current of a transformer is the current at its rated kVA. It sets the minimum size of conductors and overcurrent devices on each side. The tables cover standard three-phase sizes at the usual secondary voltages and at common primary voltages.

## The formula

<div class="formula">I = kVA × 1000 / (√3 × V<sub>LL</sub>)</div>

kVA is the rated apparent power and V<sub>LL</sub> the line-to-line voltage on the side you are checking. Full-load current is independent of the load power factor, because kVA already includes it. The <a href="/transformer-full-load-current-calculator/">transformer full-load current calculator</a> gives both sides at once for any rating. For a single-phase unit, divide kVA × 1000 by the voltage.

## Worked example

A 500 kVA transformer with a 480 V secondary has a full-load current of 500,000 / (1.732 × 480) = 601 A, matching the chart. The same transformer on a 208 V secondary has 1,388 A. The lower the voltage, the larger the bus, cable and breaker needed for the same kVA.

On a 13,800 V primary the same 500 kVA unit draws 20.9 A, so the primary side is a small cable and a modest fuse. This large ratio between sides is also why the primary and secondary protection are chosen separately.

## What the number is used for

- **Conductor and device sizing.** NEC Article 450 sets the maximum overcurrent protection as a percentage of the full-load current, and the percentage depends on the voltage and on whether protection is on one or both sides. Check the current edition for the exact figures.
- **Inrush.** On energizing, a transformer can draw many times its full-load current for a short time. Protective devices must ride through it.
- **Fault level.** The fault current at the secondary follows from the full-load current and the impedance. See the <a href="/transformer-short-circuit-current-chart/">transformer fault current chart</a>.

## Questions

### What is the full-load current of a 1,000 kVA transformer at 480 V?
1,203 A on the secondary.

### What is the full-load current of a 75 kVA transformer at 208 V?
208 A.

### Do the primary and secondary currents multiply to the same kVA?
Yes. 500 kVA at 13,800 V gives 20.9 A, and the same 500 kVA at 480 V gives 601 A. The power is equal on both sides, ignoring the small losses.
