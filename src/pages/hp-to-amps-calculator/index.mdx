Horsepower does not convert to amps with one formula, because the current also depends on the voltage, the motor's efficiency and its power factor. That is why the code uses a table. This calculator gives you both numbers: the NEC table value that is used to size circuits, and an estimate of the current the motor actually draws at full load.

## Two different answers

<div class="formula">Table: Table 430.250 (three-phase) or 430.248 (single-phase), by hp and voltage<br/>Estimate, three-phase: I = hp × 745.7 / (√3 × V × efficiency × PF)<br/>Estimate, single-phase: I = hp × 745.7 / (V × efficiency × PF)</div>

**The table current** is a standard value for each horsepower and voltage. The NEC uses it to size the conductors and the short-circuit protection, whatever is on the nameplate. It is deliberately a little higher than a typical motor draws.

**The estimate** applies the formula, with the efficiency and power factor you enter. It is an approximation of the running current at full load. For a real motor, the nameplate current is better, and it is the figure used to set the overload protection.

## Worked example

A 10 hp three-phase motor on 460 V:

- **Table current:** 14 A.
- **Estimate** at 90 % efficiency and 0.85 power factor: 7,457 / (1.732 × 460 × 0.9 × 0.85) = 12.2 A.

The estimate is lower than the table value, which is normal. The next step, the conductor, uses the table: 1.25 × 14 = 17.5 A. The <a href="/motor-circuit-calculator/">motor circuit calculator</a> carries on from here to the overload and the breaker.

## Common mistakes

- **Using the estimate to size the circuit.** Under the NEC the conductor and the short-circuit device are sized from the table current.
- **Using the table to set the overload relay.** The overload setting comes from the nameplate current of the motor you have.
- **Choosing the wrong voltage column.** A 480 V supply normally uses the 460 V column, and a 240 V supply the 230 V column.
- **Expecting the nameplate to match.** A nameplate can show a different figure from the table, and that is fine. The table is a sizing basis, not a measurement.

## Questions

### How many amps is a 5 hp motor?
On a three-phase 460 V supply the table gives 7.6 A. On 230 V three-phase it is 15.2 A, and a single-phase 230 V motor has 28 A.

### How many amps is a 1 hp motor?
The table gives 2.1 A at 460 V three-phase, 4.2 A at 230 V three-phase, and 8 A at 230 V single-phase.

### Why is the table current higher than my measured current?
The table is a standard value used for sizing. A real motor's current depends on its efficiency, power factor and how heavily it is loaded, so a lightly loaded motor can draw much less.

### Can I use this for a motor with a drive?
Motors on variable-speed drives have their own requirements. Check the drive manufacturer's data and the NEC provisions for adjustable-speed drives.

## Motor circuit sizing — the full NEC sequence

Under NEC Article 430, a motor circuit has four components, each with its own sizing rule:

1. **Branch circuit conductors (NEC 430.22):** rated at 125 % of the full-load table current. For a 10 hp / 460 V motor (table current 14 A): 14 × 1.25 = 17.5 A minimum conductor ampacity. 12 AWG copper at 75 °C covers 20 A — adequate.

2. **Short-circuit and ground-fault protection (NEC 430.52):** sized from Table 430.52. For an inverse-time breaker, up to 250 % of table current for a standard motor. 14 × 2.5 = 35 A — use a 35 A breaker. If starting trips the breaker, up to 400 % is permitted under NEC 430.52(C)(1). Never confuse this device with running overload protection: it only opens on a short circuit or ground fault.

3. **Overload protection (NEC 430.32):** set at 115–125 % of the nameplate full-load current (not the table value). For a nameplate of 13.5 A: set the relay at 13.5 × 1.25 = 16.9 A.

4. **Disconnecting means (NEC 430.102):** rated at 115 % of the full-load table current minimum. For 14 A: 14 × 1.15 = 16.1 A — a 20 A disconnect is the standard choice.

**Key point:** the breaker is intentionally oversized to allow the motor to start. The overload relay protects the motor against sustained overload. These two devices do different jobs and are sized by different rules.

## Table current versus nameplate current

The NEC motor sizing rules use two different currents for two different purposes:

| Use | Current to use | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Conductor sizing | 125 % × table current | NEC Tables 430.248 / 430.250 |
| Short-circuit protection | Up to 250 % × table current | NEC Table 430.52 |
| Overload relay setting | 115–125 % × nameplate current | Motor nameplate |
| Disconnect sizing | 115 % × table current | NEC 430.110 |

The table current is a standard value that is a little higher than typical measured current. The nameplate current is the measurement for the actual motor installed. Using the wrong one is a common mistake, and it usually results in overload relays that nuisance-trip or fail to protect.

## More Q&As

### What is the full-load current of a 3 hp, 230 V single-phase motor?
NEC Table 430.248 gives 17 A.

### How many amps is a 25 hp, 460 V three-phase motor?
NEC Table 430.250 gives 34 A. The conductor must be rated 34 × 1.25 = 42.5 A, so 8 AWG copper at 75 °C (50 A ampacity).

### Why not calculate I = hp × 745.7 / (√3 × V) instead of using the table?
You can estimate this way, but the NEC requires using the table current for circuit sizing. The table accounts for typical efficiency and power factor variations and provides a standard consistent basis. Your actual motor's nameplate current may be higher or lower, which is why the table is used for circuit sizing and the nameplate is used for the overload relay.

### How does a variable-speed drive change the circuit sizing?
A VFD limits the starting current to roughly 150 % of full-load current instead of the 500–800 % of a direct-on-line start. This often allows a smaller short-circuit protection device. The drive's input current may exceed the motor's nameplate current, so size the input circuit from the drive's input nameplate or the manufacturer's data, not from the motor's nameplate.
