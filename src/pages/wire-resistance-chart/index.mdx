This chart lists the diameter, cross-sectional area and DC resistance of AWG conductors. Resistance is given for copper and aluminum at 20 °C and at 75 °C, a common conductor rating. The values are calculated for a solid round conductor from the AWG definition and the resistivity of each metal.

## How it is calculated

<div class="formula">d = 0.127 × 92<sup>(36 − n)/39</sup> mm<br/>R = ρ × L / A, with ρ(T) = ρ<sub>20</sub> × (1 + α × (T − 20))</div>

The AWG number n gives the diameter d, and the area is π d² / 4. The resistivity ρ at 20 °C is 0.017241 Ω·mm²/m for copper and 0.028264 for aluminum. The temperature coefficient α is 0.00393 per °C for copper and 0.00403 for aluminum. The <a href="/awg-to-mm2-converter/">AWG to mm² converter</a> gives the diameter and area for any gauge.

## Worked example

A 14 AWG copper cable runs 200 ft to a load, so the loop is 400 ft of conductor. At 20 °C the resistance is 2.525 Ω per 1,000 ft × 0.4 = 1.01 Ω. At 10 A that loses 10.1 V, which is 8.4 % of a 120 V supply. With the conductor at 75 °C the resistance rises to 3.071 × 0.4 = 1.23 Ω and the drop to 12.3 V. Moving to 10 AWG (0.999 Ω per 1,000 ft at 20 °C) cuts the loss to 4.0 V.

## Differences from published tables

- **Stranding.** Stranded conductors are a few percent higher in resistance than solid ones of the same size, because the individual wires follow a slightly longer path. Code and manufacturer tables for stranded wire give the higher figure. Use those for a final design.
- **Coating.** Tinned or coated copper has slightly higher resistance than bare.
- **AC resistance.** At 60 Hz, large conductors have a small skin effect, and reactance adds to the impedance. For long, large conductors use an impedance table.

## Using it

Resistance is the starting point for voltage drop, power loss and fault current. Power lost in a conductor is I² × R, so a 10 A load through 1.01 Ω wastes 101 W as heat. The <a href="/voltage-drop-chart/">voltage drop chart</a> turns these resistances into volts per amp and maximum lengths.

## Questions

### What is the resistance of 12 AWG copper wire?
1.588 Ω per 1,000 ft at 20 °C, or 1.932 Ω at 75 °C, for a solid conductor.

### Why does resistance go up as the AWG number goes down?
A smaller AWG number is a thicker wire with more area, so its resistance is lower. The numbers run backwards: 4/0 is the thickest and 24 the thinnest on this chart.

### How does temperature change the resistance?
Copper rises by about 0.4 % per °C. From 20 °C to 75 °C that is about 22 %.
