Under the IEC 60364 method, a cable is selected in three steps: find the tabulated current rating it needs once installation conditions are accounted for, check it against the protective device, and check voltage drop. This calculator does the arithmetic for steps one and three. It does not contain the rating tables or the correction factors, which belong to the standard, so you enter the factors from the tables for your installation.

## The method

<div class="formula">I<sub>b</sub> ≤ I<sub>n</sub> ≤ I<sub>z</sub><br/>I<sub>z</sub> = I<sub>t</sub> × k<sub>1</sub> × k<sub>2</sub> × …<br/>so I<sub>t</sub> ≥ I<sub>n</sub> / (k<sub>1</sub> × k<sub>2</sub> × …)</div>

I<sub>b</sub> is the design current of the circuit. I<sub>n</sub> is the rating of the protective device. I<sub>z</sub> is the current-carrying capacity of the cable in its actual conditions. I<sub>t</sub> is the tabulated current rating for the cable type and installation method, at reference conditions. The k factors correct for ambient temperature, grouping with other circuits, soil thermal resistivity, depth of burial and similar effects. The overload protection rule also requires the current that makes the device operate within its conventional time, I<sub>2</sub>, not to exceed 1.45 I<sub>z</sub>. That condition is met automatically by standard circuit breakers, but it is not met by every type of fuse.

## Worked example

A three-phase circuit has a design current of 55 A, protected by a 63 A breaker. The cable runs in an ambient where the temperature factor is 0.87, grouped with others for a factor of 0.80.

k = 0.87 × 0.80 = 0.696.
I<sub>t</sub> ≥ 63 / 0.696 = 90.5 A.

Look in the tabulated ratings for the cable type and installation method and pick the smallest size whose rating is at least 90.5 A. The calculator does not choose it because the value depends on the insulation (PVC or XLPE), the number of loaded conductors and the method.

## Voltage drop

For a three-phase circuit, the resistive drop is √3 × I × L × ρ / A. The calculator takes the line-to-line voltage and uses a conductor temperature of 70 °C, which is the working temperature for PVC insulation, and reports the smallest standard size that stays within the limit you set. For the example above with 50 m of copper, a 3 % limit at 400 V needs 8.2 mm², so 10 mm² is the next size, with a drop of 2.5 %. Typical limits are in the range of 3 to 5 %, but national rules and project specifications differ, so use the one that applies to you.

The drop calculation here is resistive only. For large conductors, the reactance of the cable adds to the drop at lower power factors, and a full calculation uses the cable's impedance from the data sheet.

## Final selection

The cable must satisfy all of these:

1. A tabulated rating at or above the figure above.
2. A cross-section at or above the voltage-drop result.
3. A fault withstand check. The cable must survive the fault current for the time the protection takes to clear it, which is the adiabatic check S ≥ √(I²t) / k. The k constant depends on the insulation and conductor, with values of 115 for PVC-insulated copper and 143 for XLPE copper commonly used. This check is not in the calculator.

Take the largest of the three.

## Common mistakes

- **Applying factors to the design current instead of dividing the device rating.** The derating is applied to the cable's capacity, which is the same as dividing the requirement.
- **Using the 20 °C resistivity for a loaded cable.** The calculator uses 70 °C.
- **Forgetting the grouping factor for cables sharing a tray or duct.**

## Questions

### What is a typical grouping factor?
It depends on the number of circuits and the arrangement. The tables in IEC 60364-5-52 give values that fall as more loaded circuits are grouped together. Read them from the standard.

### Why divide by the factors instead of multiplying?
The factors reduce the cable's capacity, so the tabulated rating at reference conditions must be larger by the inverse of the combined factor.

### Does this work for NEC installations?
No. The NEC uses ampacity tables with different correction factors. Use the <a href="/breaker-size-calculator/">breaker size calculator</a> and the NEC tables.

## IEC 60364 derating factor reference

Common correction factors from IEC 60364-5-52. These are indicative values; always consult the full standard tables for the specific installation method and conditions.

**Temperature correction factors (k₁) for XLPE cable (reference 30°C):**

| Ambient temperature | PVC insulation | XLPE insulation |
|---|---|---|
| 25°C | 1.04 | 1.02 |
| 30°C | 1.00 | 1.00 |
| 35°C | 0.94 | 0.96 |
| 40°C | 0.87 | 0.91 |
| 45°C | 0.79 | 0.87 |
| 50°C | 0.71 | 0.82 |
| 55°C | 0.61 | 0.76 |

**Grouping factors (k₂) for cables on a cable tray:**

| Number of circuits | Touching | Spaced (one diameter) |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 1.00 | 1.00 |
| 2 | 0.80 | 0.85 |
| 3 | 0.70 | 0.79 |
| 4 | 0.65 | 0.75 |
| 5 | 0.60 | 0.73 |
| 6 | 0.57 | 0.72 |
| 9 | 0.50 | 0.68 |
| 12 | 0.45 | 0.66 |

Grouping factors apply only to circuits that are loaded simultaneously. A circuit that runs only occasionally does not contribute to group heating and may be excluded.

## Worked example: 55 A design current with two derating factors

A 400 V three-phase circuit has a design current of 55 A and is protected by a 63 A MCCB. The cable is PVC-insulated copper, runs at 40°C ambient, and is grouped in a tray with two other loaded circuits (touching).

**Step 1 — combined derating factor:**
k₁ (temperature, PVC, 40°C) = 0.87.
k₂ (grouping, 3 circuits touching) = 0.70.
k = 0.87 × 0.70 = 0.609.

**Step 2 — required tabulated rating:**
It ≥ In / k = 63 / 0.609 = 103.4 A.

**Step 3 — select cable from IEC 60364-5-52 Table B.52.4 (method E, three-phase):**
35 mm² PVC copper: 110 A tabulated. ≥ 103.4 A. ✓
25 mm² PVC copper: 86 A. Not sufficient.
Select **35 mm²**.

**Step 4 — voltage drop check:**
Run length 80 m, 400 V three-phase, 3% limit. R (35 mm² at 70°C) = 0.627 Ω/km.
Vdrop = √3 × 55 × 0.08 × 0.627 = 4.78 V = 1.2% of 400 V. ✓

**Step 5 — fault withstand (adiabatic):**
Assume 10 kA fault, 0.1 s clearing. Required S = 10,000 × √0.1 / 143 = 22.1 mm². 35 mm² ✓.

**Final selection: 35 mm² copper PVC**. All three criteria are met.

## Standard IEC cable sizes (mm²)

Standard cross-sections per IEC 60228 (Class 2 stranded copper):
1, 1.5, 2.5, 4, 6, 10, 16, 25, 35, 50, 70, 95, 120, 150, 185, 240, 300, 400, 500, 630 mm².

Always step up to the next standard size when the calculated area falls between standard sizes. For example, a calculated requirement of 42 mm² selects 50 mm² (not 35 mm²).

## Protective device coordination

The IEC 60364 method requires three conditions are all satisfied:

| Condition | Rule | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| Ib ≤ In | Overload | Device rating ≥ design current |
| In ≤ Iz | Overload | Cable capacity ≥ device rating |
| I₂ ≤ 1.45 × Iz | Overload protection | Device trips before cable overheats |

The third condition (I₂ ≤ 1.45 Iz) is automatically satisfied by circuit breakers to IEC 60898, since their conventional tripping current I₂ = 1.45 In. For gG fuses, I₂ = 1.6 In, which may require upsizing the cable: a 100 A gG fuse needs Iz ≥ 160 / 1.45 = 110.3 A, not just 100 A.

## More Q&As

### How do I find IEC installation method (A, B, C, E, F) for my cable run?
IEC 60364-5-52 Annex A defines installation methods by reference number (A1, A2, B1, B2, C, D1, D2, E, F, G). The method depends on how the cable is physically installed: in a thermally insulating wall (A), in conduit on a wall (B), clipped direct (C), in free air on a cable tray (E), or other. Each method has its own tabulated current column.

### Can I apply more than two derating factors?
Yes — multiply all applicable factors together. If the temperature factor is 0.87, the grouping factor is 0.70, and there is a soil thermal resistivity factor of 0.90 for a buried section, the combined factor is 0.87 × 0.70 × 0.90 = 0.548. Divide the device rating by this combined factor to get the required tabulated current.

### What is the difference between Iz and It in IEC 60364?
It is the tabulated current rating of the cable at standard reference conditions (usually 30°C ambient, no grouping, specific installation method). Iz is the actual current-carrying capacity in the installed conditions after all derating: Iz = It × k₁ × k₂ × … The cable design rule is that Iz must be at least equal to the protective device rating In.
