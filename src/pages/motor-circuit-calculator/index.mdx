A motor circuit has three separate sizing jobs, and each one uses a different current. The conductor and the short-circuit device are sized from the full-load current in a code table. The overload protection is sized from the nameplate. Mixing these up is the most common error, so this calculator keeps them apart.

## The three steps

<div class="formula">Conductor ampacity ≥ 1.25 × table full-load current<br/>Overload setting = 1.25 × nameplate current (1.15 × if the motor does not qualify)<br/>Short-circuit device ≤ table percentage × table full-load current</div>

**Full-load current** comes from Table 430.250 for three-phase motors and Table 430.248 for single-phase motors, by horsepower and voltage. The code directs you to use these tabulated values for conductor and short-circuit protection sizing, not the figure on the nameplate. The tables list the voltages 208, 230, 460 and 575 V for three-phase and 115 and 230 V for single-phase, which this calculator offers.

**Conductors** carry at least 125 % of the table current (430.22) for a single motor with continuous duty.

**Overload protection** uses the nameplate current: 125 % for a motor with a service factor of 1.15 or more or a marked temperature rise of 40 °C or less, and 115 % for other motors (430.32). The maximum setting is 140 % and 130 % respectively. If the motor still trips at those, check the load before raising anything.

**The short-circuit and ground-fault device** (430.52) has a maximum, which depends on its type: 250 % for an inverse-time circuit breaker, 175 % for a dual-element fuse, 300 % for a non-time-delay fuse and 800 % for an instantaneous-trip breaker. If the result is not a standard size, the next higher standard size is permitted.

## Worked example

A 10 hp, 460 V, three-phase motor with a nameplate current of 13 A:

- **Table current:** 14 A.
- **Conductors:** 1.25 × 14 = 17.5 A or more.
- **Overload:** 1.25 × 13 = 16.25 A, with a maximum of 18.2 A.
- **Inverse-time breaker:** 250 % × 14 = 35 A, which is a standard size.
- **Dual-element fuse:** 175 % × 14 = 24.5 A, so 25 A.
- **Non-time-delay fuse:** 300 % × 14 = 42 A, so 45 A.

A 5 hp, 230 V motor has a table current of 15.2 A, so the conductor needs 19 A and the breaker maximum of 38 A rounds up to a 40 A standard size.

## What this does not cover

- **Several motors on one circuit**, or a motor with other loads, which follow 430.24 and 430.53.
- **Variable-speed drives and soft starters**, which have their own requirements and manufacturer instructions.
- **Motor-type rows in Table 430.52.** The percentages here are the general case. The code table has other rows for particular motor types, for example a higher instantaneous-trip percentage for some energy-efficient Design B motors and lower percentages for wound-rotor and direct-current motors. Check the row for your motor.
- **Higher device ratings** that the code allows when the first choice cannot carry the motor's starting current.
- **Disconnecting means, controllers and the conductors from the controller to the motor**, each with its own rule.
- **Voltage drop**, which matters for starting. Use the <a href="/voltage-drop-calculator/">voltage drop calculator</a>.
- **Conductor size itself.** The calculator gives the ampacity needed. Use the <a href="/wire-size-calculator/">wire size calculator</a> to choose the conductor, with derating.

## Common mistakes

- **Sizing the conductor from the nameplate.** Use the table value.
- **Setting the overload from the table.** Use the nameplate.
- **Reading 250 % as a target.** It is a ceiling. A smaller device that still starts the motor is better.
- **Using a table voltage the motor is not on.** A 480 V supply is usually served by the 460 V column.

## Questions

### What is the full-load current of a 5 hp motor?
It depends on voltage and phase: 7.6 A at 460 V three-phase, 15.2 A at 230 V three-phase and 28 A at 230 V single-phase.

### Why does the code use a table and not the nameplate?
Motors with the same horsepower can have different nameplate currents. The table gives a consistent basis for sizing circuits, while the overload device protects the actual motor.

### What size breaker for a 3 hp motor?
At 230 V three-phase the table current is 9.6 A, so the inverse-time breaker maximum is 250 %, or 24 A, which is not a standard size. The next higher standard size is 25 A.

### Does a motor need a separate overload device?
Generally yes. The short-circuit device protects the circuit and the overload device protects the motor.

## Motor circuit sizing reference table — NEC 430 (three-phase, 460 V)

| Horsepower | Table FLC (460 V) | Min conductor (A) | Max inv-time breaker (A) | Max dual-element fuse (A) | Overload at 125% NP |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 hp | 2.1 A | 2.6 A | 5.3 A (std: 6 A) | 3.7 A (std: 4 A) | 1.25 × NP |
| 3 hp | 4.8 A | 6.0 A | 12 A (std: 15 A) | 8.4 A (std: 10 A) | 1.25 × NP |
| 5 hp | 7.6 A | 9.5 A | 19 A (std: 20 A) | 13.3 A (std: 15 A) | 1.25 × NP |
| 10 hp | 14 A | 17.5 A | 35 A (std: 35 A) | 24.5 A (std: 25 A) | 1.25 × NP |
| 15 hp | 21 A | 26.3 A | 52.5 A (std: 60 A) | 36.75 A (std: 40 A) | 1.25 × NP |
| 20 hp | 27 A | 33.8 A | 67.5 A (std: 70 A) | 47.3 A (std: 50 A) | 1.25 × NP |
| 25 hp | 34 A | 42.5 A | 85 A (std: 90 A) | 59.5 A (std: 60 A) | 1.25 × NP |
| 50 hp | 65 A | 81.3 A | 162.5 A (std: 175 A) | 113.75 A (std: 125 A) | 1.25 × NP |

NP = nameplate current. Overload uses nameplate, not table value. Inverse-time breaker maximum = 250% × table FLC. Next standard breaker size up is used when calculation is not a standard size (NEC 240.6(A)).

## Worked example: 25 hp, 460 V, three-phase motor

Nameplate: 32 A, SF 1.15, 460 V, 3-phase, continuous duty.

**Step 1 — table current (Table 430.250):** 34 A.

**Step 2 — conductor minimum:** 1.25 × 34 = 42.5 A. Per Table 310.16, 8 AWG copper THHN at 75°C = 50 A, so 8 AWG minimum. If conduit run is long, check voltage drop with the voltage drop calculator.

**Step 3 — overload protection (NEC 430.32):** SF ≥ 1.15 → use 125% of nameplate: 1.25 × 32 = 40 A. Maximum allowed: 1.40 × 32 = 44.8 A, so a 40 A heater or relay in the motor starter.

**Step 4 — inverse-time circuit breaker:** 2.50 × 34 = 85 A. Next standard size = 90 A. Install a 90 A breaker.

**Step 5 — dual-element time-delay fuse (alternative):** 1.75 × 34 = 59.5 A → 60 A fuse.

**Step 6 — disconnect means (NEC 430.110):** rated not less than 115% of motor FLC: 1.15 × 34 = 39.1 A. A 60 A fusible disconnect or 60 A non-fused switch satisfies this.

## NEC 430 motor circuit components

| Component | NEC article | Sizing basis | Key rule |
|---|---|---|---|
| Branch circuit conductors | 430.22 | 125% × table FLC | Minimum ampacity |
| Overload protection | 430.32 | 125% × nameplate (115% for other motors) | Protects motor from sustained overload |
| Short-circuit device | 430.52 | ≤ 250% × table FLC (inv-time breaker) | Protects circuit on fault |
| Disconnecting means | 430.102–110 | ≥ 115% × table FLC | Must be in sight of motor |
| Controller | 430.83 | Rated for HP and voltage | Motor starter or contactor |
| Ground fault protection | 430.96 | Per 430.96 or equipment listing | May be part of motor controller |

## Motor starting methods and their effect on circuit sizing

| Starting method | Starting current | Circuit impact |
|---|---|---|
| Across-the-line (DOL) | 600–700% FLC | Highest starting current, largest short-circuit device |
| Star-delta | ~200% FLC | Reduced start, motor must be wired for star-delta |
| Soft starter | Adjustable, typically 200–400% FLC | Reduces breaker and conductor stress |
| Variable frequency drive (VFD) | ≤ 150% FLC | Lowest starting current; VFD input requires separate sizing |

A VFD draws its input current from the supply at the VFD's rated input, not from the motor's starting characteristics. Size the branch circuit for the VFD's rated input current per the VFD manufacturer's data, not the motor table.

## More Q&As

### What if my breaker trips when the motor starts?
First check the breaker is a standard inverse-time type, not an instantaneous one. If it still trips, the code allows increasing the breaker to the next higher standard size, up to the 250% maximum (NEC 430.52(C)(1) Exception). For a motor that still trips at the maximum, switch to a dual-element time-delay fuse, which handles starting surges better than a breaker.

### How do I size the wire for a motor fed through a variable frequency drive?
The VFD output wiring (VFD to motor) is sized from the motor's table FLC × 1.25, same as a direct-connected motor. The VFD input wiring (panel to VFD) is sized from the VFD manufacturer's rated input current, which may differ from the motor FLC because VFD efficiencies and input power factors vary.

### Can a single circuit serve multiple motors?
Under NEC 430.53, several motors of 1 hp or less may share a branch circuit if each has its own overload and the circuit is protected by a device not exceeding the value allowed for the smallest motor on the circuit. For larger motors, separate circuits are almost always required. See 430.24 for the total circuit ampacity when more than one motor is connected.
